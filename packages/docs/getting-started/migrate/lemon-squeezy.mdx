Lemon Squeezy closed new sign-ups on November 16, 2026 and stops processing payments, including subscription renewals, on March 31, 2027. Creem is a Merchant of Record like Lemon Squeezy, so you keep selling worldwide without registering for sales tax or VAT yourself. Your AI agent can do most of the move for you.

| Date              | Lemon Squeezy                                                                                  |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| November 16, 2026 | Stopped accepting new stores                                                                   |
| Mid-January 2027  | Recommended latest date to start migrating. Lemon Squeezy says the move can take up to 8 weeks |
| March 31, 2027    | Stops processing payments, including subscription renewals. No extension is planned            |

Sources: [Saying goodbye to Lemon Squeezy](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/blog/saying-goodbye-to-lemon-squeezy) and the [Lemon Squeezy migration guide](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/migration).

## Before you start

Four checks, about 5 minutes:

1. **What you sell is allowed.** Creem reviews every account before it takes live payments. See [account reviews](/merchant-of-record/account-reviews/account-reviews).
2. **Your country gets payouts.** Creem pays out to merchants in 143 countries and territories. See [supported countries](/merchant-of-record/supported-countries).
3. **Your prices can be in USD or EUR.** If your Lemon Squeezy store uses another currency, decide your new prices first.
4. **Your customers can pay by card.** Creem's checkout takes cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay, but not PayPal. Lemon Squeezy can't move PayPal subscriptions to any provider, so those subscribers pay by card on Creem. See [payment methods](/merchant-of-record/finance/payment-methods).

## With your AI agent

This works with Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or any coding agent that can read web pages and make HTTP calls. Your agent builds your store in a Creem sandbox, a test store it creates for you, and you claim it with one link. Nothing is charged, emailed or cancelled, and your subscribers keep being billed by Lemon Squeezy.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Put your Lemon Squeezy API key in your environment">
    Create an API key in Lemon Squeezy under **Settings → API**, with test mode off. Set it as
    `LEMONSQUEEZY_API_KEY` in the shell or `.env` your agent uses. Never paste it into the chat. You
    don't need a Creem key: your agent creates the sandbox and its key.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Point your agent at the Creem skill">
    Open your agent and paste:

    ```text
    Read https://creem.io/SKILL.md and move my Lemon Squeezy store to Creem.
    My Lemon Squeezy API key is in LEMONSQUEEZY_API_KEY. Never print, log or commit it.
    ```

    `SKILL.md` is Creem's main agent skill. It sends your agent to the migration guide, [`MIGRATE-LEMONSQUEEZY.md`](https://creem.io/MIGRATE-LEMONSQUEEZY.md), and also teaches it the rest of Creem: checkout, webhooks, license checks and the CLI, which it needs to switch your app over afterwards.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the plan, then let it run">
    Your agent reads your Lemon Squeezy store and shows you every product, discount, customer count,
    subscription and license key it will bring over, and what can't move. After your yes, it builds
    them in the sandbox.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Claim your store">
    Your agent saves a claim token and gives you a report. Open [creem.io/claim](https://creem.io/claim),
    sign in and paste the token within 7 days. The sandbox becomes your Creem store, with everything in
    it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## With the Creem CLI

For your catalog, the [Creem CLI](/code/cli) copies products, discount codes, license key settings and files in one command. It reads your Lemon Squeezy store and the Creem store you're logged in to, prints a plan of what it creates, updates and skips, and writes only after you confirm. Your Lemon Squeezy API key stays on your machine.

```bash
brew tap armitage-labs/creem
brew install creem
creem login --api-key creem_test_YOUR_KEY
creem migrate lemon-squeezy --dry-run
creem migrate lemon-squeezy
```

| Option                | What it does                                                                  |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `--ls-api-key <key>`  | Your Lemon Squeezy API key                                                    |
| `--ls-store-id <id>`  | The Lemon Squeezy store to read                                               |
| `--dry-run`           | Prints the plan and writes nothing                                            |
| `--yes`               | Writes without asking. Required when no terminal is attached                  |
| `--adopt [answers]`   | Answers adoption proposals (below)                                            |
| `--exclude-discounts` | Copies everything except discount codes                                       |
| `--json`              | Prints the plan as a JSON report. With `--yes`, runs it and prints the result |

### Running it again

Every object the CLI writes carries a key in `external_id`: `ls_variant_<id>` for products and `ls_discount_<id>` for discounts. A second run finds them by that key, updates what changed in Lemon Squeezy and leaves the rest alone, so a run that stopped halfway, or a run in test mode followed by one with a live key, is safe to repeat. When nothing changed, the plan lists every item as unchanged and nothing is written.

A Creem product without a key that has the same name as a variant, ignoring case and spacing, or a discount with the same code, is proposed for adoption. The plan shows how each candidate differs, for example its price or currency, and never picks one for you. In a terminal the CLI asks about each candidate: yes gives it the key, no to all of them creates a new product. Without a terminal, answer with `--adopt ls_variant_123=prod_abc` to adopt that product, `--adopt ls_variant_123=new` to create a new one, or `--adopt` alone to adopt every proposal that has exactly one candidate. `--adopt ls_variant_123=prod_abc` also adopts a product without a key whose name differs, for example one you renamed. `--yes` never answers a proposal: while one is unanswered, the run writes nothing and exits with `7`, and so do `--dry-run` and `--json` without `--yes`, so a script knows the run would stop. An answer the CLI can't apply, for a key the plan doesn't have, a product that already has a key, or a product another answer already adopts, stops the command with exit code `2` before it asks or writes anything.

### What the CLI copies

- One product per variant: name (product name plus variant name when a product has several), description as plain text, price and currency, one-time or recurring, billing cycle (weekly and other cycles become a custom cycle), trial in days, and tax category.
- Free one-time variants as free products, and pay-what-you-want one-time variants with their minimum and suggested price.
- License key settings as a license key feature, with the activation limit and expiry.
- Files as file downloads on the product. A re-run uploads a file again only when its contents changed. The API can't show the contents of a file you uploaded yourself, so a file of yours is never taken as the Lemon Squeezy file, even with the same name: the Lemon Squeezy file is added next to it, and the plan says so.
- Published discount codes. A code limited to some variants applies to those products; any other code applies to every migrated product. A code with a redemption limit gets what's left of it, because Creem counts redemptions from zero: the CLI reads how many are used in Lemon Squeezy. While Lemon Squeezy still sells, a re-run lowers the limit to match, but never below what Creem has already redeemed.

It skips, with the reason in the plan: free subscriptions, usage-based and tiered prices, billing cycles Creem doesn't support, pay what you want with a minimum below 1.00, draft variants, draft and expired discount codes, codes whose redemption limit is used up, codes longer than 14 characters, files that couldn't be downloaded, and the hidden default variant of a product that has other variants. Discount names longer than 40 characters are shortened. Pay-what-you-want subscriptions get their minimum price as a fixed price, because pay what you want is one-time only in Creem. Images aren't copied.

A Creem product that carries a variant's key but isn't migrated any more, because the variant was deleted or is now a draft, stays as it is and is listed with an `orphaned` warning. Archive it in Creem if it shouldn't sell. The same goes for an active Creem discount whose Lemon Squeezy code was deleted, or is now a draft, expired or used up: buyers can still redeem it, so expire or delete it in Creem if they shouldn't. The CLI never removes products, features, files or products from a migrated discount. When Lemon Squeezy no longer applies a discount to a migrated product, the CLI can't tell that from a product you added in Creem, so the product stays on the discount with a `discount_product_kept` warning; remove it in Creem if the discount shouldn't apply.

The plan also lists the product groups to create: one per Lemon Squeezy product with two or more subscription variants, so customers can switch between them. The API can't create product groups yet, so create those in the dashboard. Customers, subscriptions, orders and issued license keys aren't migrated; the [technical guide](/getting-started/migrate/lemon-squeezy/technical-guide) covers them.

<Warning>
  Each run, dry runs included, downloads every published file once to compare it with the copy in
  Creem. Lemon Squeezy allows 10 downloads of a file per day from one IP address.
</Warning>

With `--json`, the report lists every item with its action, its Creem ID, and the reason or error. The exit code is `0` when every item went through, `2` for invalid options, an `--adopt` answer that can't be applied, or a missing API key, `4` when part of the Lemon Squeezy store couldn't be read, `6` when the run finished with failed items, and `7` when an adoption proposal is unanswered, also for `--dry-run` and a `--json` plan. Nothing is written with `2`, `4` or `7`. Fix what the report names and run the command again.

## What moves

| Lemon Squeezy                                | On Creem                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Products, prices, discount codes             | Moved                                                                                         |
| Customers, with their country                | Moved                                                                                         |
| License key settings                         | Moved                                                                                         |
| License keys                                 | Imported with the same codes. Your app keeps checking keys on Lemon Squeezy until you go live |
| Active and trialing subscriptions            | Imported paused, set to renew on the Lemon Squeezy renewal date. Lemon Squeezy keeps billing  |
| Files, product images, affiliates            | Set up again in the dashboard. Your agent's report lists them                                 |
| Saved cards, order history, referral history | Don't move                                                                                    |

This is what your AI agent brings over. The Creem CLI copies products and discount codes only.

**Existing subscribers** keep paying Lemon Squeezy for now. Switching each one to Creem, with their card entered once and their first Creem charge on their Lemon Squeezy renewal date, comes next. Until then you can move a subscriber with a checkout, close to their renewal date, so nobody pays twice. See [Move existing subscribers](/getting-started/migrate/lemon-squeezy/technical-guide#6-move-existing-subscribers).

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do my subscribers need to enter their card again?">
    Yes, once. Your agent imports each subscription paused, with its Lemon Squeezy renewal date, and
    the subscriber adds a card on Creem when switching opens. Creem's first charge falls on that
    renewal date, so nobody pays twice for the same period.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can my customers keep their license keys?">
    Yes. Your agent imports them with the same codes, without their activations: each device
    activates once against Creem after your app update. Keep checking keys against Lemon Squeezy
    until you go live. Creem's license check needs your API key, so it runs from your server.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What happens to my order history?">
    It stays in Lemon Squeezy. Export it as CSV before March 31, 2027. Creem shows only the orders
    it processes.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does importing customers notify them?">
    No. Creating a customer in Creem sends no email and creates no order.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do I need to finish before March 31, 2027?">
    Move new sales well before then. Every subscriber should have moved by that date: Lemon Squeezy
    stops processing renewals on March 31, 2027 and doesn't plan to extend it.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
